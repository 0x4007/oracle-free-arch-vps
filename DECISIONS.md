# Implementation decisions

Recorded 2026-09-22 for the online Backblaze backup path in this repository. These are implementation decisions made under the owner-controlled [PROJECT-VISION.md](PROJECT-VISION.md) charter. They do not amend, replace, or claim the authority of that charter, which wins on any conflict, and they stay inside the resource-budget and request-transport scope described here.

## Per-phase resource limits

Backup transient units carry these systemd limits per phase: capture/worker read 10 MB/s, verifier read 30 MB/s, write 10 MB/s for both, read/write IOPS 500/200, `CPUWeight=1`, `CPUQuota=100%`, `Nice=19`, `MemoryMax=1 GiB`, `MemorySwapMax=0`, and no `MemoryHigh` (the soft cap was removed in `429b29d` after it throttled a real working set of about 1,078 MB into a stall).

Why the verifier read differs: this 10.36 GB generation (10,355,748,786 B ciphertext) incurs about 64.9 GB of full-file verifier read passes for ciphertext hash, decryption, `zstd -t`, plaintext hash, inventory, and 4 full boot samples. At the shared 10 MB/s read cap, verification alone needs about 108 min and does not fit the 6 h gate; 30 MB/s read with the unchanged 10 MB/s write cap gives about 47.5 min and puts a normal whole cycle at about 300 min (capture 114 + planning/upload ~121 + reconstruct 17 + verify ~47.5), about 60 min inside the deadline. That is headroom for a normal cycle, not a guarantee for every retry or a larger payload.

Observed 2026-09-22: the accepted generation's verifier ran about 67 min (07:07:20.158Z to 08:14:11.145Z), including reconstruction and the initial period under the 10 MB/s read cap before the live verifier-only 30 MB/s runtime override at 07:27:39Z. The 30 MB/s verifier read is now the source default selected from the validated unit kind; worker/capture and prune units keep 10 MB/s.

## Request transport and retry behavior

Each Backblaze request now has a 120 s timeout that applies to the fetch and the response-body read together and is combined with any caller abort signal, so either the timeout or a caller cancellation aborts the request; retry paths log sanitized phase checkpoints that give the allowed operation, error category, and timing instead of raw request detail. The existing bounded retry policy is retained unchanged: 3 attempts with 30 min spacing inside the request's `GATE_DEADLINE_MS`, which the timeout does not extend.

No new environment variable, CLI flag, or configuration surface was introduced for either decision; the limits come from existing unit properties and the timeout is internal to the storage client.

Related receipt: [BACKUP-VERIFIED-2026-09-22.md](BACKUP-VERIFIED-2026-09-22.md).

## 2026-10-02 update: 12 h future request budget and 20% shared CPU quota

Observed reason: the 2026-10-02 catch-up attempt's capture ran 15:44:36Z to 20:39:40Z (4 h 55 min) and included the external 2 MB/s disk caps until they were removed at 19:31:49Z, so it is not a clean 10 MB/s baseline; upload planning continued until 21:07:23Z. During sampled transfer the cadence was about 67 s per 64 MiB PUT plus full readback, which forecasts about 3.6 h of transfer for the 12.91 GB payload; no source CPU or disk bottleneck was continuously evident in those samples, and the disk override is not claimed to be the cause of the observed cloud transfer rate. The six-hour budget recorded above is an implementation choice, not a `PROJECT-VISION.md` requirement, and it left no bounded headroom once that transfer forecast was combined with capture and verification. This update does not pre-judge the still-live attempt; its outcome is whatever its terminal status proves.

Future requests therefore use `GATE_DEADLINE_MS` derived from `GATE_DEADLINE_HOURS = 12`; the validators and their messages derive from that value, while the coupled Pi unit `config/backblaze-file-backup.service` sets `TimeoutStartSec=13h` by manual coupling to the same 12 h budget, not by programmatic derivation. The 12 h value is future bounded headroom, not a proven cycle duration: only a fresh full cycle can show that it fits.

The recorded `CPUQuota=100%` default is superseded for the shared worker/verifier launch: it now carries `CPUQuota=20%`, persisting the owner-approved 20% CPU retention. Worker read/write stay 10 MB/s and the verifier-only read stays 30 MB/s. Unchanged: the 120 s HTTP timeout, 3 attempts with 30 min spacing, receipt and full-readback checks, gate proof conditions, catalog retention and format, source coverage, encryption and private keys, and no new environment variable, CLI flag, or configuration surface.

Cutover: catalog entries carry no request or deadline fields, so existing catalog entries remain valid under the new validator. A stored job envelope still carries its exact deadline, and the new validator accepts only the new exact duration, so this candidate applies to future requests only and must not be installed over an unsettled old job. Old 6 h requests and dated evidence such as [BACKUP-VERIFIED-2026-09-22.md](BACKUP-VERIFIED-2026-09-22.md) stay as recorded and are readable with their old releases.

Expired-timeout gate reconciliation: an orphaned gate with `orphanReason: "TERMINAL_PROOF_MISSING"` and an expired immutable deadline may be cleared only by a fresh proof showing the exact unit `failed/failed` with `Result=timeout`, matching unit/status/gate invocation ids, `MainPID`/`ControlPID` 0 with no processes below, a free source lock, a known nonterminal source status, the original `finishedAtUtc: null`, and coherent non-future heartbeat/updated timestamps; the reconciliation preserves the source status verbatim, never rewrites it to `FAILED`, never synthesizes a finish timestamp, and creates no accepted backup receipt.

## 2026-10-02 update: upload pacing and hourly progress observations

A deterministic reproduction showed that the upload pacer clipped earned transfer credit to one second (4 MiB) although it charges after a 64 MiB PUT or readback. Even a transfer taking longer than its sixteen-second bandwidth allowance therefore added approximately fifteen seconds of sleep. The burst now matches one actual 64 MiB chunk; the sustained 4 MiB/s shared upload/readback ceiling and all exact-version readback, hashes, encryption and journal checks remain. Approximately 193 chunks in the failed 12.91 GB generation could incur about 96 minutes of unnecessary waits under the former logic; this is a calculated avoidable delay, not a measured end-to-end improvement. The already-running request keeps its original immutable release; the correction applies to future requests.

The owner requested hourly progress checks and detection of work unlikely to finish within a day. A VPS oneshot observer now runs hourly and records source phase, exact job cgroup CPU/IO counters, archive sizes and verified upload journal bytes. A heartbeat alone is excluded. No observed work for an hour is classified as a stall requiring inspection; unavailable counters are unknown. Upload ETA uses observed verified-byte throughput and a finalized capture size, and is explicitly upload-only because verification follows. An upload forecast beyond 24 hours from the original request is behind schedule; capture and verification have no fabricated ETA. Only fresh alarms matching the current job and invocation affect the existing Pi notification path.

The observer never creates a snapshot, kills a worker, starts a fresh capture or rewrites a request. Existing resumable transfer retries and their journals remain controller-owned. An off-pace estimate alone is not authority to discard a healthy capture. The currently running twelve-hour request and its deadline are unchanged; an overall-timeout replacement is not claimed by this observer. Source verification acceptance and controller cleanup remain separate states.

Deno requires unrestricted permissions for direct /sys reads. The observer instead permits only /usr/bin/cat and invokes it for fixed validated cgroup cpu.stat/io.stat paths; it receives no unrestricted Deno grant. The managed observation is bounded by TimeoutStartSec=60, CPUQuota=10%, MemoryMax=128M and scoped metadata/report paths. No production service restart is involved.

Worker and verifier launches now use CPUQuota=25% and MemoryMax=1536M; CPUWeight=1, Nice=19, MemorySwapMax=0 and the absence of MemoryHigh remain. Worker reads/writes stay at 10 MB/s, verifier reads at 30 MB/s with writes at 10 MB/s, and IOPS remain 500 read / 200 write. The shared upload/readback ceiling remains 4 MiB/s with the corrected one-chunk pacer.

The local codex-metrics timer is alive. Its retained CSV begins September 3, has a September 7–20 gap and one malformed row, and recent seven-day samples show RAM used around 3.6 GiB median, 4.5 GiB at p95 and 8.4 GiB peak out of 11.65 GiB, leaving at least about 3.2 GiB available in that window. An older September 23 UTC overload left about 0.8 GiB available with all 4 GiB of swap occupied. The Pi's separate minute collector has complete September 26–October 2 UTC coverage. Local load is not CPU utilization, and retained Oracle hypervisor CPU values have unresolved normalization semantics; neither is treated as a calibrated CPU percentage. The quarter-core profile is an initial conservative budget, not a promise of a four-hour cycle. Acceptance remains an observed full capture/upload/verification cycle within the request budget.

The current worker received a live resource override without a restart: its PID 530251 and invocation 6a20ef1d91954990bbdf789696bd80fc stayed unchanged, and its actual cgroup now reports a 250 ms/s CPU quota, 1,610,612,736-byte memory max, zero swap allowance and no soft ceiling. The current request and immutable release are unchanged. A task-owned runtime drop-in for this exact job's future verifier applies the same approved profile while the current Pi controller still has its earlier launcher loaded. A bounded cold-unit check proved that the drop-in overrides the earlier generated limits. New source defaults apply after the serialized controller release selection; the prior queued selector is superseded without changing its frozen candidate.

## 2026-10-02 owner override: half-resource backup budget and retained experiment telemetry

The owner explicitly requested half of all host resources, superseding the quarter-core/1.5 GiB recommendation. Live metadata confirms A1 Flex with 2 OCPUs, 12 GB provisioned RAM and 2 Gbps networking. The observed MemTotal is 12,511,203,328 bytes. Worker/verifier budgets are now CPUQuota=100% (one of two cores), MemoryMax=6,255,601,664 bytes (half MemTotal, rounded down to a page boundary by cgroup), MemorySwapMax=0 and no MemoryHigh. CPUWeight=1 and Nice=19 remain as production-yield scheduling controls; limits are ceilings, not reservations.

Fresh OCI volume API reads show a 150 GB root and a 50 GB staging boot volume, both at 10 VPUs/GB with automatic tuning disabled. Official Balanced performance is 60 IOPS/GB and 480 KB/s/GB: root 9,000 IOPS / 72 MB/s, staging 3,000 IOPS / 24 MB/s theoretical. To bound combined read plus write at half, each direction receives root 18 MB/s / 2,250 IOPS and staging 6 MB/s / 750 IOPS. The root is resolved by `/`; staging uses its verified persistent SCSI identity, matched through UUIDs and lsblk rather than a `/dev/sd*` assumption. Source identifiers must be reconciled again after any storage replacement. Future worker/verifier launches share these budgets. Network PUT and full GET readback share 125,000,000 bytes/s, exactly 1 Gbps of the configured 2 Gbps link, with one bounded 64 MiB pacer burst. Future verifier reconstruction reads use the same sustained ceiling through its existing store factory seam.

The current worker's CPU/RAM/IO properties were raised live without changing its PID, invocation, immutable request or capture. Its loaded af1 release still declares the former 4 MiB/s network pacer. That JavaScript closure cannot be changed through current supported interfaces, and restarting would violate source/gate invocation binding; no restart or forged status was performed. The 1 Gbps default therefore applies to future launches after serialized release selection. A runtime drop-in carries the half-resource budget to this job's later verifier.

Each hourly progress report now records the exact job, invocation, phase, request source revision, actual cpu.max/cpu.weight, memory hard/soft/swap caps, per-device io.max and the pinned immutable release's declared network budget. Unknown and unlimited values remain distinct. Reports append to `/var/lib/arch-vps-backup-progress/history/YYYY-MM-DD.jsonl` without deleting history; the latest report remains atomic. A limit change starts a new performance comparison baseline so mixed profiles do not produce a misleading rate. `/var/lib/arch-vps-backup-progress/experiments.jsonl` preserves the earlier source-declared profile and actual observed quarter-core and half-resource evidence, with separate provenance and recording/observation timestamps; missing past measurements are not fabricated.

Two download body-read failures occurred before the half-resource override. An isolated, bounded, read-only probe of the exact seventh 64 MiB root chunk completed and verified in 10.779 seconds under the new CPU/RAM budget on October 3 at 02:45:51 UTC. The probe records headers and byte timing and ended with zero processes. This proves that object is readable in the bounded check, not the cause of previous failures or full-cycle success. The production worker's ordinary resumable third attempt remains unchanged.

## 2026-10-03 readback failure recovery

The c812 generation ended FAILED / UPLOAD_FAILED at 03:32:09.866 UTC after 11 verified chunks; no verifier ran and the four accepted catalog generations remained. Its first two logged failures were download body-read failures; the final exception was not retained by that old release, so no precise final cause is invented. The source unit exited with Result=success despite FAILED source status, which is not backup acceptance. Terminal status, zero processes, free source lock and absent controller gate were independently reconciled before recovery.

Upload and verifier callers now retry an exact immutable GET version at most three times, with one- and two-second delays, only for closed recognized transient body-read/network/HTTP 429/5xx failures. They never retry a PUT, change object identity, refresh inventory as part of this helper, or retry integrity/authentication/404 errors. Existing request timeouts and whole-cycle bounds remain. This avoids a whole-generation replan and thirty-minute cooldown for a single transient readback failure. The transport retains response timeout signals and emits only fixed safe category, elapsed time, expected/received bytes, abort state and whitelisted exception names; credentials, URLs, file IDs, provider bodies/messages and stacks are excluded. Focused fault injection, redaction and full fake-transport tests passed.

Recovery preserves the failed controller/job/status/journal/capture metadata and all accepted cloud backups. Only task-created ciphertext staging from the conclusively failed c812 job is eligible for scratch cleanup to make room for a corrected run; source production data and unrelated legacy recovery files are excluded. A new request receives its own immutable identity and the corrected release. Capture, cloud upload/readback, archive verification and final acceptance remain separate checkpoints.
